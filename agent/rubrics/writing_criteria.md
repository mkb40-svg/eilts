# Writing assessment criteria (project wording)

This is the project's own summary of how IELTS Writing is assessed. It paraphrases the publicly
available band descriptors in our own words; consult the official public band descriptors on the
IELTS website for the authoritative text. Bands go from 0 to 9 in steps of 0.5. Each criterion
counts equally within a task.

## 1. Task Achievement (Task 1) / Task Response (Task 2)

- **9** — Every part of the task is fully addressed. Task 2: a clear, consistent position with
  fully developed, well-supported ideas. Task 1 Academic: a clear overview plus well-chosen key
  features. Task 1 General: purpose fully clear, every bullet point fully covered, tone exactly right.
- **8** — All parts addressed well; only occasional minor lapses in development or detail.
- **7** — All parts addressed. Task 2: clear position throughout, main ideas extended and
  supported, though some may over-generalise. Task 1 Academic: a clear overview, key features
  highlighted and illustrated, could be more fully extended. Task 1 General: all bullets covered and
  highlighted, tone consistent.
- **6** — All parts addressed but some more fully than others. Task 2: relevant position, some
  conclusions unclear or repetitive; ideas relevant but some insufficiently developed. Task 1
  Academic: an overview attempted, key features adequately covered, some detail irrelevant or
  inaccurate. Task 1 General: bullets covered, tone may be uneven.
- **5** — Task only partly addressed; format may be inappropriate in places. Position unclear or
  not always kept; ideas limited, not well developed, possibly repetitive. Task 1: no clear
  overview, mechanical recounting of detail, or missing key data.
- **4** — Minimal or tangential response; position hard to identify; ideas few, unclear, or
  irrelevant. Task 1 may confuse key features with detail.
- **Under length** (Task 1 < 150 words, Task 2 < 250 words) limits this criterion: the response
  cannot fully address the task. Off-topic or memorised text scores very low.

## 2. Coherence and Cohesion

- **9** — Cohesion is effortless and unnoticeable; paragraphing is skilful.
- **8** — Information and ideas sequenced logically; cohesion managed well; paragraphing
  sufficient and appropriate.
- **7** — Logical organisation with clear progression; a range of cohesive devices used
  appropriately, some under- or over-use; each paragraph has a clear central topic.
- **6** — Coherent overall progression; cohesive devices used effectively but some faulty or
  mechanical linking; referencing not always clear; paragraphing not always logical.
- **5** — Some organisation but no overall progression; inadequate, inaccurate or overused
  linking words; repetitive due to weak referencing; paragraphing may be missing or inadequate.
- **4** — Information not arranged coherently; basic or inaccurate linking; no clear paragraphing.

## 3. Lexical Resource

- **9** — Wide range used naturally and precisely; very rare slips only.
- **8** — Wide range used fluently and flexibly to convey precise meaning; skilful use of
  less common items; rare errors in word choice, spelling or word formation.
- **7** — Sufficient range for flexibility and precision; some less common items with awareness
  of style and collocation; occasional errors in choice, spelling or word formation.
- **6** — Adequate range for the task; attempts less common vocabulary with some inaccuracy;
  some errors in spelling or word formation that do not impede communication.
- **5** — Limited range, minimally adequate; noticeable errors in spelling or word formation
  that may cause some difficulty for the reader.
- **4** — Basic, repetitive vocabulary; limited control of word formation or spelling; errors
  may strain the reader.

## 4. Grammatical Range and Accuracy

- **9** — Wide range of structures with full flexibility and accuracy; rare slips only.
- **8** — Wide range; the majority of sentences are error-free; only very occasional errors.
- **7** — A variety of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences; good control of
  grammar and punctuation with a few errors.
- **6** — A mix of simple and complex sentence forms; some errors in grammar and punctuation
  that rarely reduce communication.
- **5** — Limited range; complex sentences attempted but less accurate than simple ones;
  frequent errors that can cause some difficulty for the reader.
- **4** — Very limited range; rare subordinate clauses; errors predominate and punctuation is
  often faulty.

## Calibration notes for the examiner

- Judge each criterion independently; do not let one weakness drag down all four.
- A band 7 essay can contain errors; a band 9 essay contains only rare slips.
- Count frequency: occasional errors (7), some errors (6), frequent errors (5).
- Memorised phrases that do not fit the question do not earn lexical credit.
- When in doubt between two bands, choose the lower one and say why in the comment.
